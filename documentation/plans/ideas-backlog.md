# Ideas backlog (2026-11-17)

All ideas for the website that are not part of the plan yet. None is committed. Each can become a GitHub issue.

- **Part 1** (S1 to S74): ideas from the first take of the spec.
- **Part 2** (1 to 158): ideas from a separate ideation pass. They repeat none of part 1.

- Tags: `[quick]` cheap and static, `[build]` real work, `[server]` needs a running service, `[wild]` ambitious or speculative.
- Reference an idea as "backlog S12" (part 1) or "backlog 12" (part 2).
- Some ideas of part 2 assume note properties that were not checked: birth years on person notes (16, 68), external ratings, credits and ids on media notes (17, 20, 68), quote verification data (126).

## Part 2: ideas from the ideation pass

## A. Discovery and navigation

1. [build] Three-way compass. Each note ends with three computed links: "Deeper" (strongest outgoing link by shared neighbors), "Wider" (the map of content that holds the note), "Elsewhere" (a far note in another cluster). The reader gets a decision, not a list of 20 related notes. Static, from the link index.
2. [quick] Part-of chips. A line under the title lists the maps of content that contain the note. The 270 maps hold about 182 links each, so almost every note gets a way up. Static.
3. [quick] Visited trail. A small script keeps the notes of the session in `localStorage` and shows "Your trail" in a drawer, with export as a Markdown link list. The reader can retrace a rabbit hole and save it to a vault. Client script, no server.
4. [quick] Unread by design. `:visited` styles in hubs, backlinks and library grids show which of the 20,000 notes the reader opened. Returning readers see what is left. CSS only.
5. [quick] New since your last visit. The header shows "43 new, 6 in topics you read", from a stored timestamp and a small daily changes file. It gives a reason to come back without an account. Client script plus one static JSON file per day.
6. [build] Time-boxed tours. Each topic offers "5 minutes", "30 minutes" and "an afternoon" itineraries, built from reading time and the link order in the maps. A newcomer in front of 20,000 notes gets a sized commitment. Static.
7. [quick] Question index. The build collects notes with a question title or a defining `description` into "Questions this garden answers" pages by topic, with FAQ structured data. It is an entry for people who arrive with a problem. Static.
8. [quick] Alias index. An A to Z index lists the aliases as entries ("PKM: see Personal Knowledge Management"). 5,385 notes carry aliases, so the garden gets an encyclopedia-style entry point. Static.
9. [build] Bridge pages. One generated page per topic pair with enough shared notes, for example "Where AI meets knowledge management". The 22 top topics give up to 231 pairs, and each page is also a search landing page. Static.
10. [build] Concept ladders. Per topic, the build orders notes into levels 101, 201 and 301 from link direction and in-degree, with author overrides in the map. Readers follow a thread in the right order. Static.
11. [quick] Collection-aware previous and next. A quote links to the next quote of the same author, a movie to the next of the same year or director, a person to the next in the same field. Wandering continues inside a collection. Static.
12. [quick] Hatnotes. "Not to be confused with" and "See also" lines come from title similarity and shared aliases. At 20,000 notes, near-duplicates confuse readers and search engines. Static.

## B. What only this content makes possible

13. [build] Idea lineage ribbon. A permanent note shows one line: source, literature note, this note, article, newsletter, product. The reader sees where the idea came from and where it went. Static, from typed edges in the link index.

15. [quick] The people who shaped this garden. A ranking of the 1,000 people by citations in permanent notes and by quote count. It is an intellectual family tree that people share and argue about. Static.

17. [build] Taste profile. Rating distributions per decade, genre and medium, plus "where I disagree with the crowd". It is the Letterboxd stats page for four media types at once. Static SVG charts.
18. [build] What should we play tonight. A picker over the 2,375 board games: players, duration, weight, owned, with a shareable URL. Board game groups return to such a tool and link to it. Static JSON plus a small client filter.
19. [quick] Backlog burn-down. The stats give movies 2,056 of 4,890 done, video games 77 of 1,994, board games 24 of 2,358. The page says "at this pace, the movie backlog ends in year X". It is honest and funny. Static, from the `stats.json` history.
20. [build] Credit and year pages. Virtual pages for directors, designers, studios and release years ("1999 in my library"), with average ratings. They are generated pages, not vault notes. Static.
21. [build] Practice room. Tabs and scores get a tempo control, bar loops, a metronome and a count-in. A chord-change drill uses the chord notes, and a streak sits in `localStorage`. The music notes become a tool that a learner opens daily. Client script, loaded at the first Play.
22. [build] Reverse chord finder. The reader clicks frets or keys and gets the chord name and its note. The chord note properties are the lookup table. It is a link magnet for guitar and piano learners. Small client script.
23. [build] Topic drift chart. A stacked area chart of notes per topic per month since 2022 shows the move from software development to knowledge management to AI. It tells the 4-year story in one picture. Static SVG from the `created` dates.
24. [build] The notes behind this product. Each product gets a page with the public notes that match its tags ("read these 212 notes free, then get the system"). Each of those notes links back. Static, from the catalog and the link index.
25. [quick] Newsletter issue in context. Each of the 231 issues lists the notes created or tended in that week ("what I was learning when I wrote this"). The archive becomes a door into the garden. Static.
26. [build] Compare your vault. A reader enters numbers, or drops a file from a small free plugin command, and sees them next to this vault: notes, links per note, tags per note, types. It is a lead for the Obsidian Starter Kit. Client only.

48. [quick] Scoped search. "Search inside this map of content, this person or this folder", through Pagefind filters made at build time. Static.
49. [build] Video transcript search. YouTube transcripts enter the index with time-coded links, and each result names the related note. Video content becomes findable from the garden. Static.

58. [build] Inline definitions. The first unlinked mention of a term that has a note becomes a `<dfn>` with a popover that shows the description. It is opt-in per type and capped per note. Static, with the Popover API.

60. [quick] Copy menu for Obsidian users. Copy as wikilink plus URL, as a callout quote with the source, or "for AI" with frontmatter and neighbor titles. It fits how his audience works. Small client script.
61. [build] Read aloud. Text-to-speech at build time for permanent notes and articles, as an `<audio>` element and a podcast feed. Files are cached by content hash. It also shows the voice side of the brand.
